Slides 1–12 • Electrical system

The framework everything else depends on.

Core rule

Interpret in the same order

P waves → PR → QRS → regularity → rate → rhythm.

Measurements

Numbers

Small box 0.04 s • large box 0.20 s • normal PR 0.12–0.20 s.

Pacemaker hierarchy

Intrinsic rates

SA 60–100 • junction 40–60 • ventricular backup 20–40.

Mechanism

Generation vs conduction

Sinus arrest = SA node fails to generate impulse. SA exit block / AV block = impulse is generated but conduction fails.

Slides 13–33 • Sinus rhythms

Ask: is the SA node still in charge?

Slide 16

Normal sinus rhythm

60–100, regular, upright matching P before every QRS, constant PR 0.12–0.20, narrow QRS.

Sinus brady

Sinus bradycardia

Same sinus pattern, rate <60.

Sinus tach

Sinus tachycardia

Same sinus pattern, rate >100. Think cause/demand, not just rate.

Sinus arrhythmia

Sinus arrhythmia

Sinus P waves stay consistent but R–R spacing varies, commonly with respiration.

Slide 26

SA exit block

Whole sinus cycle drops out; pause tends to be an exact multiple of baseline P–P.

Sinus arrest

Sinus arrest

SA node does not fire. Pause is usually not an exact multiple of prior P–P.

Slides 34–45 • Junctional rhythms

The most important discriminator is timing.

Slide 37

PJC

Premature = early. Early narrow QRS; P may be inverted before, hidden in, or after QRS.

Slide 40

Junctional escape beat

Escape = late. Appears after a missed/delayed higher pacemaker impulse.

Junctional escape rhythm

40–60/min

Several sequential junctional beats with the same retrograde/hidden P clues.

Accelerated junctional

60–100/min

Same junctional origin, faster than the usual escape range.

Junctional tach

>100/min

Junctional origin with tachycardic rate.

Slides 46–64 • Atrial rhythms

Look closely at P-wave organization.

PAC

Premature atrial complex

Early atrial beat with an abnormal/different P morphology; QRS usually narrow.

Slide 56

Atrial fibrillation

No organized P waves + irregularly irregular R–R.

Flutter

Atrial flutter

Organized rapid atrial activity, often sawtooth. Around 300 atrial/min with 2:1 conduction gives ventricular rate around 150/min.

WAP

Wandering atrial pacemaker

At least 3 different P-wave shapes, irregular, rate <100.

MAT

Multifocal atrial tachycardia

At least 3 P morphologies like WAP, but tachycardic, conventionally >100.

CompareKey difference
AF vs MATMAT still has discrete P waves of multiple shapes; AF has no organized P waves.
Flutter vs SVTFlutter around 300 atrial/min with 2:1 conduction can create a ventricular rate around 150. Look carefully for flutter waves.

Slides 65–85 • Re-entry, SVT & WPW

Mechanism matters more than memorizing a shape.

SVT

Supraventricular tachycardia

Mechanism originates above the ventricles; common narrow-complex paroxysmal examples include AVNRT and orthodromic AVRT.

Slides 70–71

AVNRT

A PAC can find the fast pathway refractory, travel down the slow pathway, then return up the recovered fast pathway to sustain a loop.

AVRT

Accessory pathway re-entry

The re-entry circuit uses an accessory pathway outside the normal AV nodal route.

Slides 81–82

WPW triad

Short PR + delta wave + widened QRS.

Slides 86–105 • AV blocks

Follow every P wave and ask whether it conducts.

1st degree

First-degree AV block

Every P conducts; PR is prolonged >0.20 s and constant.

Mobitz I

Second-degree type I / Wenckebach

PR gets longer, longer, longer, then QRS drops; cycle resets.

Mobitz II

Second-degree type II

Conducted PR intervals stay constant, then a P suddenly has no QRS.

3rd degree

Complete heart block

P waves and QRS complexes march independently: AV dissociation, no fixed PR relationship.

Exam traps / lecture corrections

These are the places most likely to trip you up.

No P ≠ automatically AF

P can be hidden, retrograde, or difficult to see. AF requires the overall pattern.

PJC vs escape

PJC is early; junctional escape is late after a pause.

2:1 block trap

A 2:1 conduction pattern by itself does not prove Mobitz II.

Complete block ≠ PEA

PEA requires organized electrical activity without a pulse. You need patient assessment.

WAP vs MAT

Both have ≥3 P shapes. The main separator is rate: WAP <100, MAT >100 conventionally.

QRS boundary

Your deck often treats ≤0.12 s as narrow; clinically ≥0.12 s is commonly treated as wide. Follow instructor convention for quiz wording.
