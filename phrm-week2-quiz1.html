Week 2 Review

Learn the directive wording in chunks, then attach the numbers.

Trauma Cardiac Arrest

CPR

Cardiac arrest secondary to severe blunt or penetrating trauma • CPR in 2-minute intervals.

Contraindications: obviously dead as per BLS PCS or meets DNR conditions.

Manual defibrillation

Age >24 hr • LOA altered • VF or pulseless VT. Rhythms other than VF/pulseless VT are not defibrillated under this item.

Trauma TOR

Core: age >16 • no palpable pulse • no defibrillation delivered. Then the applicable sign-of-life/transport branch: no signs of life since fully extricated, OR signs of life when fully extricated with closest ED >30 min, OR PEA with closest ED >30 min.
Do-not-TOR situations: age <16 • defibrillation delivered • signs of life since fully extricated • PEA with closest ED <30 min • penetrating torso/head/neck trauma with lead trauma hospital <30 min.

TXA • Traumatic Hemorrhage

Indication: suspected hemorrhage due to trauma AND hemodynamic instability.

Conditions: age ≥16 • HR ≥110 bpm or hypotensive.

Contraindications: TXA allergy/sensitivity • >3 hours from injury or unknown injury time • isolated head injury.

Dose in your review: 1000 mg split into 2 doses.

Course update: no TXA for traumatic VSA.

ROSC Checklist

  1. Note time of ROSC.
  2. Repeat primary survey.
  3. Delegate egress planning.
  4. Monitoring: defib pads, cardiac monitor, ETCO₂, SpO₂.
  5. Ventilation: consider advanced airway; ~10 breaths/min; ETCO₂ 30–40 mmHg.
  6. Oxygen: SpO₂ 94–98%.
  7. Hemodynamics: BP every 5 min; consider 2nd IV.
  8. STEMI: consider 12-lead about 10 min after ROSC and STEMI activation as appropriate.
  9. Reassess en route and update receiving hospital.

Reversible causes

H's: hypoxia, hypovolemia, hydrogen ion/acidosis, hypo-/hyperkalemia, hypothermia/hyperthermia. T's: tension pneumothorax, tamponade, thrombosis, toxins.
